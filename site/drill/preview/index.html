    Device Lab · Reference

    Talk phones like a pro

    Customers don't buy spec sheets — they buy someone who can explain, in one sentence, why this phone fits them. These are the words on the box, translated.

    The spec words, translated
    RAM

    The phone's short-term memory

    RAM is where running apps live. More RAM (6–12GB) = more apps open at once without stutter or reloading. It is not storage — closing apps doesn't free up photo space.

    Sell it as: "keeps WhatsApp, banking and camera running smoothly at the same time."

    Storage

    The cupboard — 64 / 128 / 256 / 512GB

    Where photos, videos, apps and WhatsApp backups live. 128GB is the comfortable minimum today; heavy camera users want 256GB+.

    iPhones and most new flagships have no SD-card slot — the storage they buy is the storage they keep. Ask about their photo habits before choosing.

    Battery

    mAh — the fuel tank

    5000mAh is the modern standard and gets most people through a full day. 6000mAh (Huawei Nova Y74) is the "never near a plug" choice. Fast charge = less time on the cable.

    Camera

    MP is not the whole story

    Megapixels = detail, but sensor quality and software matter as much — a 12MP iPhone can out-shoot a 108MP budget phone. OIS (optical image stabilisation) = sharp shots in low light and steady video.

    Screen

    AMOLED & 120Hz

    AMOLED = deep blacks, punchy colour, better outdoors. 120Hz = scrolling looks twice as smooth as a basic 60Hz screen. Together they make a phone feel expensive.

    5G

    Faster data — with conditions

    5G needs three things: a 5G phone, a 5G-capable SIM, and 5G coverage in the area. Where there's no 5G signal the phone simply uses 4G/LTE — nothing breaks.

    IP rating

    IP67 — dust and splash protection

    Survives rain and an accidental dunk. It is not "waterproof forever" — seals age, and water damage is still typically excluded from warranty.

    Software

    iOS · Android GMS · Huawei HMS

    iOS — iPhones only, long updates. Android with GMS (Samsung, Honor, Oppo, Vivo) — full Google Play. Huawei HMS — Huawei's AppGallery instead of Google.

    New Huawei devices have no Google Play Store and not every Google app is supported — the Usage Limit form even makes the customer sign for this. Say it before the sale, not after.

    The brands on our shelf
    Apple

    iPhone — the badge and the ecosystem

    Holds value, long software support, effortless with AirPods/iPads. "e" models (16e/17e) = the affordable door in; Pro = cameras and 120Hz ProMotion; Air = thinnest premium.

    Preowned iPhones (13/14) are the cheapest way into Apple.

    Apple carries a 12-month warranty — most Androids carry 24. It's on the KIP checklist for a reason.

    Samsung

    Galaxy — something at every price

    A-series = the volume sellers (A17→A57): 5G, AMOLED, long support. S-series = flagship (S26); FE = flagship features at mid price; Ultra = 200MP camera + S-Pen; Z Fold = the folding showpiece.

    Honor

    Spec-per-rand fighter

    X-series = tough, big-battery value (X6e, X9D). 400/600 series = camera-led mid and upper-mid. Watch for bundles — the X7e ships with a smartwatch and earbuds in the box, an easy value story.

    Honor runs full Android with Google apps — it split from Huawei in 2020.

    Oppo

    Design-led, camera-led

    A-series = solid budget/mid (A6K, A6, A6 Pro). Reno = the slim, style-and-camera line (Reno15 F, Reno15 Pro) for customers who care how the phone looks in hand. Full Google apps.

    Huawei

    Hardware heavyweight, HMS software

    Nova = value and battery (Y-series, Nova 15 Max). Pura = serious camera flagship. Build quality punches above the price.

    Runs HMS / AppGallery — no Google Play. Perfect for WhatsApp-and-camera customers; wrong for someone glued to Google apps. Qualify first.

    Vivo

    Entry-level workhorse

    Y-series (Y04): big battery, big screen, low price. An honest first smartphone or a no-fuss replacement.

    EasyForms · Reference

    The form pack, decoded

    The EasyForm workbook prints the whole pack for an order. Fill the Details sheet once and it feeds every form. Here's what each page is for — and the traps that cost people.

    Details sheet

    Fill once, feeds everything

    Customer, device, package and consultant details go in here and flow onto every form. Then print only what the order needs: pages 1–3 (KIP + waiver) for most, 4 N4L, 5 upgrade approval, 6 SIM swap, 7–11 transfer.

    CPD

    Customer Pick-up Document

    Proves the customer received the device they ordered, in good order — signed by both of you at handover.

    The CPD is not proof of purchase. The till slip / invoice is — no slip, no warranty claim. Tell every customer to keep it, plus the box and accessories.

    7 days — out-of-box failure can be exchanged/refunded. 6 months — defective goods rights under the CPA. Both need the slip.

    KIP

    The mandatory checklist

    Every contract type — no exceptions. You tick that you explained the package, fees, first-bill pro-rata, bill & debit dates, out-of-bundle rates, credit and usage limits, early termination, OBF policy, and that insurance is a separate product.

    Upgrades: the R150 (excl VAT) upgrade fee on the next bill must be explained — it's a named tick-box.

    Usage limit

    The customer's choice, the customer's job

    They choose a limit or decline one — but setting it is their responsibility, via the app or *136*5#. The store isn't liable if they skip it.

    The limit does not cover loaded bundles, me-to-you transfers or in-app purchases. This form also carries the HMS acknowledgement for Huawei devices.

    Ins. waiver

    Declining insurance, in writing

    If they don't take device insurance, they sign the waiver: their choice, freely made, device is their responsibility outside warranty.

    14 days — they can still take the insurance after purchase. After that, the door closes. Ticking "insurance" on a form is only an application — cover starts when the debit order is actually on the account.

    N4L

    Number-for-life migration

    Moves an existing prepaid number onto the contract. It's an addendum to the subscriber agreement and goes through credit vetting like any application.

    Airtime and data bought on prepaid do not carry over — anything unused when the migration goes through is lost. Tell them to use it up first.

    Upgrade

    Upgrade Approval form

    The approval is valid for 7 days and subject to stock. A new 24-month term starts. If the deal requires the old handset back, it must work — and come with battery and charger.

    SIM swap

    Consumer SIM swap

    Lost or stolen SIM? Suspend it immediately. Swap activates in 2–24 hours. Original ID / ID card / passport, or an original certified copy — never a copy of a certified copy.

    Someone else doing it for the customer needs a delegation letter (dated, signed, saying exactly what they may do) plus both parties' IDs.

    Transfer

    Contract to a new account holder

    Pages 7–11: the new client takes over the remainder of the contract. Every document must be in before it processes.

    The account holder's name and the bank account holder's name must match. The existing client stays liable up to and including transfer day.
